======================================================================
UPGRADES
======================================================================

Seven upgrades, all passive, all picked up off the wall with blood.
Two are KEPT for the run; the other five are BORROWED for 40 turns.

HOW THE ECONOMY WORKS
---------------------
  * HP is the only currency. An upgrade costs a flat 2 HP -- the same
    price for all seven. There are no tiers, no rarities, and no
    prerequisites.
  * A purchase can never take you below 1 HP, so a pickup can never
    kill you. It can only leave you somewhere you have to recover from.
  * Each upgrade is a pickup placed on the wall with its own sprite.
    Walking onto its cell takes it -- no pause, no menu. A lower-third
    pop-up names what you got and what it does, and (on your first run
    only) a narrator explains it.
  * Stepping onto a pickup you cannot afford leaves it standing, so you
    can feed and come back. Stepping onto one that would do nothing --
    Grace already capped -- absorbs it for free rather than charging
    2 HP for nothing. That is the only case left: a duplicate of a
    temporary power pays full price and buys a fresh 40 turns, because
    the one you are carrying is running out either way.
  * One pickup per segment, eight segments, each a single upgrade drawn
    at random from the seven (duplicates allowed) -- deliberately more
    blood asked than a single run can afford. The shortfall is the point.
  * Kills pay 2-6 blood, so one guard or mason funds two upgrades.

KEPT vs BORROWED
----------------
  * KEPT (no clock): Strength, Grace.
  * BORROWED (40 turns from the moment you take it): Plummet, Rend,
    Rappel, Surefoot, Ambush.

Strength and Grace answer the two things that are true for the WHOLE
descent -- the fight and the sun -- so paying blood for them is an
investment in the run. The other five change what one piece of WALL
does, and a wall you cannot climb past is a local problem. They are a
key, not a tool: take one near the thing it opens, or waste it.

40 turns against a 200-turn night, with a pickup roughly every 25 turns.
A player who takes every pickup carries one or two powers at a time and
never the full set -- which is the point, since owning all five at once
turns the wall's four refusals into no refusals at all. It is also
comfortably longer than the walk to the next pickup, so a power taken on
the way down is still running when the wall asks for it.

The clock counts turns, not uses. Resting in shade burns it just as
descending does, which is what makes an early detour for a power a real
cost rather than a free stockpile. When it runs out the power is simply
gone -- the wall goes back to refusing what it refused before -- and the
pop-up says so, because a power that vanishes silently is
indistinguishable from one that never worked. The HUD shows the turns
left on everything you are carrying.

HOW THEY STACK
--------------
  * Strength stacks linearly, without a cap, and keeps it.
  * Grace stacks, but is hard-capped at 2 free steps, and keeps it.
  * Ambush stacks linearly (+2 each), but on one shared clock: a second
    purchase doubles the damage and REFRESHES the 40 turns rather than
    extending them. Hoarding duplicates of one power is never the best
    use of a run's blood.
  * Plummet, Rend, Rappel and Surefoot are owned/not-owned while their
    clock runs. A second purchase re-arms the clock and nothing else.

The four contextual abilities never contend with each other -- each
answers a different press, and no single press can be barred two ways
at once:

    press DOWN, barred     -> Plummet
    press SIDEWAYS, barred -> Rappel
    press UP, barred       -> Surefoot
    anything else barred   -> Rend


======================================================================
THE SEVEN
======================================================================

STRENGTH -- "Strength"
  Blurb : +1 damage per bite
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: Each purchase adds +1 to bite damage, on top of the base 2.
          Stacks linearly with no cap. Combines with a banked Ambush.


GRACE -- "Grace"
  Blurb : +1 free step in sunlight, refilled by shade
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: Gives a pool of free steps that absorb the sun. Standing in
          sunlight spends one instead of burning you; only when the
          pool is empty does the burn land.
          The pool refills to full on any turn that ends in shade or
          darkness -- so it is a rhythm (short deliberate hops through
          the light), not a battery you drain once.
          Buying it fills the pool immediately, so the steps you just
          paid for are usable without finding shade first.
          Each purchase is +1 step, but the pool is HARD-CAPPED AT 2
          however many times you buy it. A third purchase is wasted.
          (The cap exists because Grace is the only upgrade that
          answers the sun directly; uncapped, it is just immunity to
          the thing the game is about.)


PLUMMET -- "Plummet"
  Blurb : Drop straight down, stopped only by walls
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: Pressing DOWN over an overhang, into a cell that isn't
          solid, drops you straight down instead of refusing the step.
          You fall past ledges and open wall alike and stop at the
          first thing solid -- rubble, a window, a creature, or the
          tower's foot.
          Rubble below counts as wall, not gap, so a fall never starts
          through one.
          Costs a single turn no matter how far you fall.
          Owned/not-owned, and TEMPORARY: 40 turns, refreshed (not
          extended) by a second purchase.


REND -- "Rend"
  Blurb : Smash the stone you climb into
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: Climbing into stone smashes it rather than being refused.
          Breaks rubble and barriers alike -- anything the step was
          refused for -- so long as the obstruction is in the cell you
          are trying to ENTER.
          A lip carried by the cell you are STANDING on is not
          smashed: you can't get a swing at the stone under your own
          feet. That's what leaves Surefoot a job.
          Windows are never smashed. A window is an opening you pass
          by, not stone to break, and it is the one thing on the wall
          Rend will not open.
          Costs a turn and DOES NOT MOVE YOU -- you clear the way, then
          step through on a later turn.
          Can destroy shade you wanted. That's on you.
          Owned/not-owned, and TEMPORARY: 40 turns, refreshed (not
          extended) by a second purchase.


RAPPEL -- "Rappel"
  Blurb : Slide down a rib you cannot step past
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: A rib bars the way across. Owning this turns that refusal
          into a slide down its face.
          You do NOT cross the rib -- you end up below it, at the same
          landing Plummet would find.
          Plummet's standing-up mirror: the same fall, started by the
          obstacle that stands up rather than sticks out.
          Owned/not-owned, and TEMPORARY: 40 turns, refreshed (not
          extended) by a second purchase.


SUREFOOT -- "Surefoot"
  Blurb : Haul yourself up over an overhang
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: A lid bars the way UP off a ledge exactly as it bars the way
          down onto one, and Plummet only ever answered the downward
          half. This hauls you up over it, one cell.
          There has to be somewhere to stand once you are up: never
          rubble, and never a window (an opening, not a ledge).
          Turns a course of barriers into something climbable instead
          of a one-way valve.
          Owned/not-owned, and TEMPORARY: 40 turns, refreshed (not
          extended) by a second purchase.


AMBUSH -- "Ambush"
  Blurb : Hold still, then strike harder
  Cost  : 2 HP
  Effect: Each purchase adds +2 damage to your next bite -- but only
          as a single armed charge.
          Waiting arms it. The charge is armed AFTER the turn
          resolves, so it belongs to your next action rather than to
          the enemy phase you just sat through.
          Moving, biting, or rending all clear it.
          Standing still longer never banks more: it is a flag, not a
          counter. The magnitude stacks with purchases (+2 each), the
          charge does not.
          TEMPORARY: 40 turns, and the banked charge lapses with it.
          Temporary rather than kept, though it is damage like Strength
          is, because a stack of it is worth having only where there is
          something to bite -- it answers a fight in front of you, not
          fighting in general.
          (Anything that paid you to keep standing still would rebuild
          the permanent safe camp the daybreak rule exists to prevent.)
          The only upgrade that answers the game's other verb. Every
          other one is paid out for moving; this one is paid out for
          not.
